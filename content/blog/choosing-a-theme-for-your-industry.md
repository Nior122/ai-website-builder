A theme is more than a color palette. It is the collection of small decisions —
palette, type pairing, spacing rhythm, corner radius, accent treatment — that
together tell a visitor *what kind of business you are* before they read a
word. Picking the wrong theme sends the wrong trust signal.

## Match theme to trust expectations

Different industries carry different visitor expectations:

- **Luxury** (fine dining, high-end hospitality): generous whitespace, serif
  display type, dark-on-light or muted gold accents, large imagery. Says
  *considered, expensive, slow*.
- **Modern** (SaaS, startups): sans-serif, high contrast, generous accent
  color, rounded corners. Says *fast, current, technical*.
- **Corporate** (law, finance, real estate): steady neutral palette, restrained
  accents, generous but not lavish spacing. Says *stable, trustworthy,
  institutional*.
- **Creative** (agencies, studios, portfolios): bold color, asymmetric layouts,
  oversized type. Says *opinionated, bespoke, design-led*.

## Type pairing

A safe, versatile pairing is a geometric sans for headings and a humanist sans
for body. If your brand leans *considered* or *editorial*, swap the heading
face for a serif. Avoid using more than two families — the moment a third
enters, cohesion drops.

## Spacing rhythm

Consistent vertical rhythm matters more than absolute values. Pick a base unit
(0.25rem or 0.5rem) and make every section use a multiple of it. The studio's
presets (SM/MD/LG) are scoped to the same unit so generated spacing always
lines up.

## Don't fight the default

Each template ships with an industry-tuned default. That default was chosen for
a reason. Make small, considered changes rather than redesigning from scratch —
you will get to a coherent result faster than starting from a blank canvas.
